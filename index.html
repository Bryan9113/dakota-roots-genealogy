About Dakota Roots

A South Dakota family helping other families find answers.

Dakota Roots Genealogy is owned by Bryan Olson and Abigail Olson. We both grew up in South Dakota and are raising our three children here. Family is at the center of our lives, and our own interest in genealogy showed us how meaningful it can be to discover where you came from and finally find answers to questions passed down for generations.

We created Dakota Roots Genealogy to help other families uncover their history, evaluate family connections, understand the people who came before them, and preserve those discoveries for children and grandchildren. Our approach is to be clear about what the records support, what is probable, and what remains unresolved.

Based in South Dakota, researching beyond South Dakota.

Common questions

Before we start your search

Genealogy is evidence-based work, and every family starts with a different set of clues.

What information do I need to start?

Whatever you have. Names, approximate dates, places, photos, obituaries, family stories, screenshots, or old documents can all provide useful starting clues.

Can you guarantee you will solve my family mystery?

No. Records can be incomplete, conflicting, restricted, or missing. We will tell you what the evidence supports and clearly identify questions that remain unresolved.

Do you only research South Dakota families?

No. Dakota Roots is based in South Dakota and has a special connection to the region, but projects can follow families across the United States and beyond when useful records are available.

What will I receive when the project is finished?

Deliverables depend on the package and findings, but may include family-tree charts, ancestor profiles, timelines, source notes, maps, military or cemetery findings, and a written or illustrated family history report.

What if records disagree with each other?

Conflicting records are common in genealogy. We compare dates, places, names, relationships, and source quality, then explain the conflict rather than hiding it.
